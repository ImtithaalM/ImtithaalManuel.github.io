Design Principles — Power Subsystem PCB

A group PCB design project focused on developing the power subsystem for a mobile robot. We designed the subsystem in KiCad while considering power delivery, current requirements, motor control and practical PCB manufacturing constraints.

What was the project?

Working as part of a group, we designed the complete power subsystem for a mobile robot, referred to as the “MM”. The project required us to apply PCB design principles to a real engineering system rather than simply designing an isolated circuit.

A major consideration was current handling. Higher-current pathways required wider copper traces to safely carry the expected load without excessive heating. This meant using electrical calculations and component specifications to make practical design decisions.

My contribution

My section of the board focused on the motor system, the ON/OFF switch and external load switching. I worked on a design capable of driving four motors bidirectionally while meeting the power requirements of the robot.

The subsystem was designed to reduce standby current to below 30 µA when switched off while still being capable of delivering the robot's required peak current during operation.

Engineering decisions

One of the most important design considerations was ensuring that the PCB could safely handle the expected current. This influenced trace widths, component selection and the physical layout of the board.

The design also incorporated practical considerations for debugging and modification. Sections were kept modular where possible, with component placement and routing designed to make testing, replacement and potential trace modifications easier.

Debugging & problem solving

One of the biggest lessons from the project came during debugging. A PCB can appear correct in the design software and still behave differently when physically assembled and tested.

This taught me the importance of designing with troubleshooting in mind from the beginning. Being able to isolate sections, replace components and modify connections made it much easier to identify and address unexpected behaviour.

What I took from the project

This project strengthened my understanding of how electrical requirements translate into physical PCB design. It also showed me that good engineering is not only about making a design work, but about making it practical to test, debug, modify and ultimately manufacture.

Working as part of a group also gave me experience coordinating different parts of a larger system and understanding how individual subsystems need to work together to achieve the overall design objective.
