About Me

Engineer by day, matcha enthusiast by choice

I'm passionate about coding, robotics, AI and renewable energy — and I love getting my hands dirty with the hardware side of things just as much as the theory. Over the last four years at UCT I've built development boards, soldered power subsystems onto veroboard, chased bugs in Simulink models, and helped a virtual “kitticopter” learn to fly (long story, see Projects).

Outside of coursework, I tutor Embedded Systems and Control Engineering, mentor first years, and have spent two years as an Orientation Leader — most recently as Head EBE Orientation Leader. I'm a fast learner, a strong communicator, and a firm believer that engineering is better (and more fun) with more women in the room.

I'm fluent in English and conversational in Turkish and Afrikaans, comfortable across Python, Java, C, MATLAB/Simulink, and I've dabbled in basic Ubuntu/Rocky cluster and VM setup for high-performance computing (more on that under Experience).

Beyond the lab

When I'm not soldering something

I try to give balance the same energy I give engineering. Zumba and Pilates are usually on the cards a few times a week — my reset button after a long day of debugging — and I firmly believe there isn't a problem, academic or otherwise, that a good matcha can't help with. I genuinely love it; anyone who knows me can confirm I have a “usual order.”

Outdoors, I'm just as content as I am at a workbench. Long walks, beach days, and hunting for gemstones and crystals are some of my favourite ways to unwind, and I'll happily lose an afternoon in a museum learning about history whenever I get the chance.

And then there's EEElite — my CHPC Student Cluster Competition team. Competing alongside those girls, and making a bit of history together, is easily one of my favourite memories from university, on or off a circuit board.
