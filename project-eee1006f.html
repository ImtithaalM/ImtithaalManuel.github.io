Water Level Warning Circuit

A hands-on digital electronics project focused on designing and building a water-level warning system using logic gates, LEDs, a buzzer and other discrete electronic components.

What was the project?

The aim of the project was to design and build a Water Level Detection System (WLDS) that could indicate the level of water in a tank and provide appropriate warnings under different loadshedding conditions.

The system produced three water-level outputs: HIGH when the tank was full, MED when it was half full, and LOW when it was empty. These conditions were then used to control a warning system consisting of LEDs and a buzzer.

What I worked on

I worked on the design and construction of the digital circuit, translating the required water-level conditions into a practical logic-gate system. The circuit had to respond differently depending on both the water level and whether loadshedding was occurring.

The final design therefore had six possible warning states. These ranged from a green LED indicating a full tank during normal operation to a red LED and beeping buzzer when the tank was empty during loadshedding.

Engineering decisions

An important part of the project was making realistic engineering assumptions while working within the design constraints. A variable resistor was used to represent the changing water level, while an LDR was used to simulate the presence or absence of power.

The design also had to remain practical, use standard components where possible, stay within the R50 additional-component budget, and operate without programmable devices such as an Arduino or Raspberry Pi.
